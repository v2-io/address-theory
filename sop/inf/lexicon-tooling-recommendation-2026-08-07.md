# Recommendation — gloss segments, term marking, and what `bin/refresh-lexicon` assumes

Written alongside `bin/refresh-lexicon` (this instance's `bin/`, destined for the verisectorium theory instance once it proves out). It exists because the instrument had to assume conventions that are not ratified: the steward's own framing of the term-marking format was *"(whatever that is)"*. Everything below is **proposed**; the script implements the proposals so their consequences are visible against a real corpus rather than argued in the abstract, and every finding it prints says so.

Two things are already settled and are not re-opened here: term entries project their definition text from the segment's tagged span (`DECISIONS.md` D5), and term marking is `[[term/x|x]]` locally / `#instance/term/x` cross-member (verisectorium `form-lexicon-realization`). What follows fills the gaps those leave.

## 1. The gloss problem, and the recommendation

**The demand.** Some segments introduce a term *pedagogically* before — or instead of — defining it formally. [[intro-postal-model]] is the live specimen: it introduces reference act, identity, location, and ambiguity narratively, in the corpus's load-bearing on-ramp, and it is the one segment a reader meets first. Those introductions must stay **fully referenceable** (citable, addressable, surfaced in the lexicon) without becoming a second definition of the same term.

**Why "just let both be definitions" fails, in this corpus's own vocabulary.** A term entry is a *designator record* whose referent is a definition atom. Two atoms answering to one entry is precisely a **collision** ([[def-descriptors]]) — the loud failure mode designators are supposed to have, and the corpus would be minting it deliberately. Worse, a gloss is discussion-grade by nature and a definition is axiomatic; letting them share a slot launders register upward, which is the drift symptom [[post-names-are-interface]] predicts ("hand-restated definitions diverging from their source").

**Recommendation — one definer, many glossers, distinct anchors.**

1. A gloss segment marks its pedagogical introduction with a **`*[Gloss (<anchor>)]*`** span — the same extraction machinery as `*[Definition (…)]*`, a different kind. The tool already recognizes the kind.
2. The term entry gains a native, repeatable **`glossed-by:`** array (segment slugs, optionally `slug#anchor`). `defined-by:` stays singular and stays the projection source. The lexicon renders the definition, then the gloss(es), each labeled, with the definition first regardless of reading order.
3. **Authority is stated, not implied:** on divergence, the definition wins; a gloss is never citable as the definition. This is the same authority chain the corpus already runs (segments > outline > matrix).
4. **Ordering stays a view property** (verisectorium `form-pedagogical-ordering`): the gloss lives where the reader meets it — inside the intro segment, in its own voice — and is *pulled* into the lexicon, never hoisted out of the segment.
5. A gloss segment declares the term in **`terms-relevant`**, not `terms-required` (it does not build on the definition; it precedes it).

**Why this is worth more than tidiness here specifically:** a term entry that carries one transcluded definition span plus N transcluded gloss spans, each addressed sub-atom and role-marked, is exactly the endpoint D5 names — a literal reference act with a transclude disposition. Gloss support turns that endpoint from a single-slot case into a *role-marked multi-destination* case, which is real demand data for `form-act-anatomy` and the projection/disposition rows, generated by this corpus's own dogfooding rather than invented for it. If the RA design cannot express "the definition span of X" and "the glosses of X" as acts, we learn that from our own lexicon before we learn it from a user.

**Not recommended:** a separate `gloss/` directory (splits the reader's segment in two and orphans the prose from its context); making glosses their own atoms (grain inflation — a gloss is not independently adjudicable); or letting the lexicon body be hand-written where a gloss exists (re-creates the paraphrase drift the whole D5 design exists to kill).

## 2. Term marking — the format, and the rule that makes a checker possible

**Format:** `[[term/<slug>]]` or `[[term/<slug>|<the words as written>]]`, per `form-lexicon-realization`. The tool also recognizes `[[#instance/term/<slug>]]`.

**The rule (proposed): first encounter per segment.** A defined term should be term-marked at its **first occurrence in a segment body**; later occurrences in the same segment are free prose. Rationale: the cost the marking pays for is per-*encounter* recognition ([[post-names-are-interface]]) — a reader needs one visible handle at the point of first meeting, and marking every occurrence buys nothing while making prose unreadable in the source, which is the medium this estate actually reads in.

**What the corpus does today** (a finding, not a criticism): term links appear *only* in the closing "Terms defined here:" declarations. In segment prose the convention is ALL-CAPS emphasis (`DESIGNATOR`, `UNIVERSAL LOCATION`). That is a perfectly good marking convention — it is just not machine-resolvable, and it means **no cross-segment term dependency is currently recorded anywhere**. The three arrays below are the repair.

**Detection is deliberately conservative.** The checker flags ALL-CAPS and bold surface forms by default, and lowercase prose ("path", "description") only under `--strict-prose`. Flagging every lowercase "path" in an addressing corpus would produce hundreds of findings and teach every reader to ignore the tool — the classic instrument that dies of noise. Longer terms suppress shorter ones (`UNIVERSAL LOCATION` is not also an unmarked `LOCATION`). Fenced code is skipped.

## 3. The three frontmatter arrays

| Array | Means | Semantics |
|---|---|---|
| `terms-required` | definitions this segment genuinely **builds on** | prerequisite; a reader who does not have this term cannot follow the segment |
| `terms-relevant` | useful or pedagogical mentions, **not** prerequisites | informative; drives "see also" and gloss placement |
| `terms-informal` | appears in the body **without definitional structure on purpose** | whitelist; silences `unmarked-mention` for that term in that segment |

They are intended as a **partition** — the tool reports a term listed in two of them. A segment's *own* defined terms belong in none of them (they are its output, not its input).

**On the names:** the steward's phrasing was `term-dependencies` / `relevant-terms` / (unnamed whitelist). I chose a common `terms-` prefix so the three sort and read together in frontmatter, and `-informal` because the array's honest meaning is "informal usage, deliberately" rather than "whitelist" (which names the mechanism, not the fact). This is a preference, not an argument — renaming is one constant in the script. The existing `depends:` array (segment slugs) is left alone; `terms-required` is its finer-grained sibling, and once populated, `depends:` could plausibly be *generated* from it — worth testing before deciding, not assumed.

## 4. What the instrument found on first run (the honest state)

37 findings against 26 terms / 8 segments. The shape matters more than the count:

- **`definition-drift` (17)** — entries are paraphrases, not projections. Already known and named in `PRACTICA.un` (`terms-alignment`: *"entries are paraphrases; the byte-identity discipline is specified, unsatisfied"*). The tool's contribution is that the gap is now located per term, so the alignment pass after steward review of the definitions is mechanical rather than a re-read.
- **`anchor-missing` (9)** — nine terms whose defining segment carries no span the entry can project from. These are mostly terms defined *inside* prose of a broader anchor (`canonical-location`, `entity`, `value-object`, `true-identity`, `working-path`, `preferred-location` all inside `def-entities-values-promises`; `progressive-routing`, `sequence-cause`, `over-determination` likewise). Fix is one of: an explicit `anchor:` in the entry, a finer `*[Definition (…)]*` span in the segment, or — the honest option in some cases — accepting that the term is defined at paragraph grain and the projection unit needs to be sub-paragraph. **That last one is a genuine design question for D5's endpoint** (how fine can a transcluded reference act address?) and I did not resolve it.
- **`unmarked-mention` (3)**, **`defines-undeclared` (2)**, **`term-unused` (2)** — small, individually actionable.

I made no edits to `src/` or `terms/`. The findings are the deliverable; acting on them is a corpus decision after the steward review that `PRACTICA.un` is already waiting on.

## 5. Open questions I did not decide

1. **Projection grain** (above) — paragraph-level anchors vs sentence-level. Blocks nine terms.
2. **Does `LEXICON.un` belong at instance root or in the lexicon dir?** Root, matching `PRACTICA.un`, is what I chose; the theory instance may prefer `terms/LEXICON.un`. One constant.
3. **Should the lexicon carry naming-decision history** (asf's `bin/term decide` event files)? Deliberately not built — this corpus has `DECISIONS.md` and no per-term decision volume yet. When a term's naming decision needs a trail, the asf pattern is proven and portable.
4. **Host-convention collision, again.** `PRACTICA.un` already flags that the udon repo uses co-located `TODO-*.md` lanes while this instance uses the verisectorium kit shape. `bin/refresh-lexicon` adds a second such datum: the udon repo has no `bin/` convention of its own, and this script assumes the estate's Ruby-instrument convention (asf `bin/*`). Recording rather than resolving.

## 6. Feedback to the verisectorium theory instance

- **`form-lexicon-realization` is load-bearing and undrafted** (outline row, `proposed`). Writing a tool against it required deciding the first-encounter rule and the three arrays, neither of which the row states. If the row drafts, these are candidate content — or candidate contradictions, which is more useful.
- **`form-generated-views`'s "clobber-guarded"** turned out to want a concrete mechanism: the script refuses to overwrite an output file that lacks a `GENERATED VIEW` marker in its first lines, `--force` overrides, and writes are temp-file + atomic rename (multi-agent safety, inherited from asf `bin/refs`). Offering the mechanism as the row's worked case.
- **`norm-affordance-default`'s "`term`-not-hand-edited-lexicon"** holds up in practice, with one amendment worth the row: the affordance that earned its keep first was not `add`/`edit` but **`--check`**. The instrument's value on day one was making an already-known gap *located*; the write half was the easy half.
