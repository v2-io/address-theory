## The short version

The `def/` layer is good, and its literature grounding holds up at the primaries: every `[SG]` claim in `defs.source.ud` and every paper citation in `def/` that I checked against the papers is accurate (list at the end). The theory is now being used outside itself, carefully. Where that use strains, the strain usually points at a real gap in the theory, and several of those gaps also show up inside the entries. Reading the papers whole sharpened most of them: for four of the five definitional defects below, the scope-graphs line already has the fix, and for the two biggest open questions (the moment, and what a resolution result *is*) it marks exactly where this theory has to go past it. The pages around the entries — ORIENT, PRACTICA, LEXICON.md, the `ref/` links — have gone stale in ways that will mislead the next agent.

## 1. On the irony: agents shown the format adopt the theory

The format is not theory-neutral, so agents are not wrong to take up the theory with it. `@{x}` is a reference; `{1,1}` is an intended cardinality; `:avoid` is collision policy; a term-group is a scope holding bindings whose names `compose-defs` resolves. The second edition said so on purpose (its D5: "this corpus its own first consumer"). Showing an agent the format is showing it the theory in miniature.

If the two need to be separable, the cheapest route is a short format spec with examples from outside addressing. ai-risk-model's `def/README.md` is already most of one: it keeps the theory's block meanings and adds `|conditions`, `|practices`, `|collisions`, `|proposed-widening`, `|ambiguity`. The other route is to say plainly, in this instance's front door, that the format uses the theory's own vocabulary deliberately.

## 2. How ai-risk-model is using the theory

The adoption there is deliberate (GAMMA plan §3.2: "DDD supplies the strategy … address theory supplies the mechanism"), pinned at `90f4fb6`, and checked by its own review loop. Its citation check confirms every theory term it cites; the de novo review and the thin pass caught most of the stretches. I'd call the reading faithful. The places it strained are more useful to this instance than the places it fit:

- **A binding to a test.** Plan §3.3 sorts a source term as either *bound* or a *match*. The thin pass (`weight-and-defects.md`, D-bound-vs-match) corrected this to two axes: who maintains the name's sense, and how that sense reaches instances. A statutory definition binds a name to a *test*, and the test then reaches risks, people or models by match. The theory does not say what happens when a binding's referent is itself a reference or a test. The papers make the gap sharper than ai-risk-model could see it: in every scope-graphs formulation, resolution ends at a **declaration** — "name resolution associates each reference to its intended declaration(s)" (ESOP 2015 §1); queries return ⟨path, declaration⟩ pairs (Zwaan 2024 §3, "Paths do not include the declaration it resolved to, but stop at the scope in which the declaration occurs"). `def-resolution` says results are **referents**. ai-risk-model's resolution records count definitions reached ("outcome: one" means one definition), which is the scope-graph sense, and reads oddly against this theory's wording.
- **Two moments, not one.** The thin pass needed `read_as_of` (when we read the source) separate from `evaluated_at` (the moment the source's test applies) — D-bitemporal. The theory has one `as-of`, and `@{moment}` is undefined. The papers give no help here: every formulation resolves against one fixed graph, and Rouvoet 2020's stability compares one graph against its extension.
- **A resolution status.** An import nobody followed must not read as "zero referents" (D-resolution-status: attempted / delegated / not needed). The second edition's D7 ("zero only from a completed perspective") covered this and did not come back. Rouvoet 2020 now gives it a precise form (§4 below).
- **"Several" apart from "ambiguity".** ai-risk-model had to invent "several", because the theory's `ambiguity` cannot distinguish them (defect 1 below).
- **A legislature's intent kept apart from its operative test** (`influx/source-atlas/SCHEMA-READING-NOTES.md:96`, on SB 53 §22757.14). This is the reach/check distinction from the archived Fable dialogue of 2026-08-08, which never came back to `def/`. The statute's purpose clause is a *check* component: material from the writer's world carried along so drift can be detected. `def-match`'s invariant ("detectable only if the reference carries something to check against") is the only trace of it in the current entries.
- **"Ambiguity" used for "we can't tell which reading was meant".** The citation check flagged that the plan uses the word in two senses: structural (more than one maximally-preferred result) and epistemic (our uncertainty about which binding the writer meant). The theory has no word for the second.
- **"Quality bar" vs `:status proposed`.** The plan calls these entries "the quality bar", which is faithful to Joseph's words about their refinement; their recorded status is still `proposed`. Worth keeping in view when ai-risk-model leans on them.
- **Import / alias bindings whose referent changes on amendment** (source-atlas feedback, `co-others.md`). Zwaan 2024 §2.2 already has the shape: a re-export "can typically be considered as a combination of an import and a declaration, where the declaration always points to the imported member", with accessibility judged at the re-export's location. That maps onto ai-risk-model's `imported` / `incorporated` binding kinds. ESOP 2015 §2.5 adds a distinction they will probably need: *import* (lower precedence than local declarations) vs ML-style *include* (same precedence — duplicates rather than shadowing).
- **"The same except" across definitions** (D-frame-inheritance: SB 53's two codes, RAISE, lineage descendants). Scopes as Types §2.3 and §2.5 model this exactly — `extends` as an edge that keeps the composed structure rather than merging eagerly, and instantiation scopes carrying explicit, lazily applied substitutions. That is a ready formal model for `inherit` + `override`, if they want one.
- **Java packages "exist by virtue of use".** `def-binding` carries this as its hardest stress case. Rouvoet 2020 §6 resolves it with the same decomposition `defs.source.ud` guessed: "compilation units query for all other compilation units in the same package and make their types accessible by adding import edges" — package membership is a *match* over contributors, and every binding stays maintainer-local. The working note can move from "stress case" to "decomposes, per the literature", with the strain on "kept by whoever minted it" recorded.

I ran ai-risk-model's builder over this `def/` (`lexicon/build --check --only …/v2/references/def`): it reads all seven files (29 terms, 7 groups) and reports the same unresolved and plural references `feedback.md` lists. In practice that builder is the missing `LEXICON.md` assembler.

## 3. Defects in the entries, with what the papers say

`feedback.md` checks out — I re-counted its numbers (ten singular `@{referent}` against the `referents` id; the plurals; the forward references; the em dash). These are beyond it:

**1. `ambiguity` has lost its tie to intended cardinality** (`def-resolution.ud:38`, "More than one maximally-preferred result"). As written, every multi-result answer to a {0,N} reference is an ambiguity, which contradicts resolution's own invariant at line 19 and `def-reference`'s miss table ("Three against {1,1} = the reference narrowed insufficiently"). The papers show why the definition arrived this way: program references always intend one declaration, so for them "more than one" and "more than intended" coincide. ESOP 2015 §1: "Ambiguous references correspond naturally to multiple resolution paths." The later formulations move the {1,1} out to the use site: in Statix the variable rule requires "the result of resolution must be a single declaration-type pair" (Scopes as Types §3), and Statix-core makes it an explicit constraint, `single(t, z)` (Rouvoet 2020 §3.2). Zwaan 2024's T-VAR: "The query should return a single result, as the name would otherwise be ambiguous." *Proposed:* ambiguity is more maximally-preferred results than the reference's intended maximum — the general form of `single`, which this theory's intended cardinality already supplies.

**2. "as of now" vs "as of a moment."** `def-reference.ud:27` defines resolve "as of now"; the intro there and `def-resolution`'s `:as-of @{moment}` say "a moment". "Now" rules out asking what something resolved to at a past moment, which the 2026-08-08 dialogue wanted first-class (timestamps and versions as check components; querying an entity's prior states). This is the same gap as ai-risk-model's two moments.

**3. A purely local hit has no representable path.** `resolution-path` (`def-resolution.ud:21–22`) says in prose that it includes "bindings consulted, matches run"; its `|rels` allow only walk or mediated steps, `{1,N}`. A binding found in the origin scope crosses no edge, so it has no path. All three formulations represent this case: ESOP 2015's path ends in a terminal `D(x)` step and may be `[] · D(x)`; Scopes as Types' rule NR-Refl gives the zero-edge path `s`; Zwaan 2024 returns ⟨path, declaration⟩ with the path stopping at the declaring scope. *Proposed:* either `{0,N}` edges plus a terminal "binding consulted" step, or the result as ⟨path, binding⟩ — the second also serves §4's result-level question.

**4. `collide` is counted in two places.** `def-binding.ud:11` counts it per maintainer; `def-scope.ud:13` counts it per scope. SB 53 (one legislature, two code chapters) is exactly the case where they disagree, and ai-risk-model tripped on it (D-collide-scope). The binding entry is written before scope on purpose, so the fix is wording: collide as "more than one mint of the same name in one jurisdiction", with `def-scope` making jurisdiction precise. The literature's evidence for the theory's position is stronger than the working note says: Scopes as Types §2.3 deliberately omits field-name uniqueness from its rules because "this can be expressed with a scope graph query that requires that a field name reference … resolves to a single declaration", and Rouvoet 2020 §2.2 writes uniqueness as an explicit `noDups` premise on the defining rule. Uniqueness as a policed norm, checked by query, is how the field already works.

**5. `def-scope`'s cardinalities don't agree, and its edge label is typed as a name.** `:enclosed-by {0,1}` with "Every scope reaches at least one root-scope" means exactly one, unless containment can be plural; acyclicity is never stated. ESOP 2015's well-formedness says the pair the entry needs: the parent "is a partial function" and "The parent relation is well-founded." Separately, `edge :label @{name} {1,1}` makes "containment" a name someone would have to bind. In every formulation labels are their own small set, a parameter of the language (Scopes as Types Fig. 1, "labels l ∈ L a set of edge labels"), not names bound in scopes. *Proposed:* a label sort of its own, and "well-founded" in the containment invariant.

Two smaller ones:

- `def-location` claims the "Exact scope-graphs correspondence" with ESOP's module. ESOP's module has three conditions — named, encapsulates declarations, *and* "can be imported into another scope" (§2.4). Location drops the third. That is a reasonable generalization, but the word "exact" should go or the dropped condition should be named.
- `def-reference.ud:35` says "(in this case a "referral act")". The term that was retired on 08-09 was REFERENCE-ACT; "referral" is a different word. Probably "referring act".

## 4. What reading the papers adds to the open questions

**Where this theory diverges from scope graphs, and why that divergence is its contribution.** In ESOP 2015, "Each reference and declaration is unique and is distinguished not just by its name, but also by its position", and each "appears in exactly one scope". A scope-graphs reference is an *occurrence*. This theory's reference is an *artifact* that can be copied and carried, with origin and moment supplied at use (`def-reference`'s invariants; the "one deliberate deferral" in the outline). Nothing in the read literature treats a reference that travels. That split — reference as artifact, use as ⟨reference, origin, moment⟩ — is worth stating outright, because it is what the moment question and the resolution-status question both grow from.

**What a resolution result is.** The literature returns declarations; this theory returns referents. Both are needed, and the ai-risk-model statutes show why: resolving "catastrophic risk" in Labor Code limb (2) reaches one *binding* (§1107(a)), whose referent is a *test* that, applied at a moment, reaches a population. `def-generator` already has the vocabulary for the second step — a deferred item can be *held* or *reached through*. *Proposed:* resolution yields bindings with their paths; reaching through a binding whose referent is itself deferred material is a further, separately determined step, with its own as-of. That one sentence would settle ai-risk-model's two axes, defect 3, and the "binding to a test" case together.

**The moment, split.** The papers resolve against one fixed graph and say nothing about time. Rouvoet 2020 is explicit that the world only grows: "scope graph facts are only asserted and never retracted" (§7.1). So the split is this theory's own to make: at minimum, the moment a resolution is performed vs the world-state it is performed against — the database sense of "as of". The 08-08 dialogue's "version is to timestamp as designator is to description" (a version is a minted moment, a timestamp a measured one) is the natural next layer.

**Resolution status, via stability.** Rouvoet 2020 §5.2 gives the exact criterion for the growing world: an answer computed in a partial graph is stable iff the extension contains no *critical edge* — an edge on a resolution path in the final graph whose source is already present, the first missing step. It also proves the operational weakening: no *weakly critical* edges (one-step lookahead against the label regex) suffices. Visibility is non-monotone: "extending a graph with additional nodes and edges may obscure—i.e., shadow—information that was previously visible" (§3.1). *Proposed reading, mine:* ai-risk-model's statuses map onto this. *Attempted* with no open critical edge is a stable answer; *attempted* with one still open is the second edition's "found-none-so-far" / `defs.source.ud`'s UNANSWERABLE-YET; *delegated* is a mediated step deliberately not taken, which leaves an open critical edge by construction. udon has the same object: a document with `result: incomplete-input` (primer §2.2) is a partial graph, and whether a reference resolved against it is stable is this criterion exactly. The shrinking world — retraction, rebinding, a dangle — is still outside every paper read, as `defs.source.ud` already records.

**Accessibility's factoring.** `def-resolution` leaves this open. Zwaan 2024 suggests a clean split by *who declares* each part. Admissibility (the label regex) and preference are declared at the use site — in Statix "shadowing behavior is specified at the use site" (Rouvoet 2020 §2.2). Accessibility is carried on the *declaration*, by its maintainer: "including accessibility information in the declaration … After resolution, we check if the path that the query returns is actually valid according to the accessibility level of the declaration" (§3). Where a route itself restricts access (C++ private and protected inheritance), Zwaan 2024 encodes it on *edges* instead (§8, `EXT_PRV` / `EXT_PRT`). And because accessibility can decide which result wins, the full path order in Appendix A.1 is a preorder over ⟨path, declaration⟩ pairs "parameterized by a scope s, which is the scope in which the reference occurs" — preference turns out to be origin-relative too. *Proposed:* asker-declared (admissibility, preference), maintainer-declared (accessibility on the binding), route-declared (restrictions on edges).

**Name lookup is itself a match.** In Scopes as Types and after, a reference finds its declaration by a data predicate over the reachable declarations — `DECL(x)`, "matches all declaration-type pairs y : T where x ≃ y" (§2.1). So a designator's connection, looked at closely, is a recognition test (the name) over the bindings in reach, plus the binding's upkeep. That is what `def-binding`'s "recognizable … nothing beyond recognizability does work" is gesturing at, and it fits `def-match`'s "the two mechanisms meet at the corners without merging." It is also the precise form of ai-risk-model's two axes: axis one finds the binding by recognizing its name; axis two is what the binding's referent reaches.

**Mediation and self-influence.** ESOP 2015 thought the Fig. 12 anomaly occurred "only in the presence of mutually-recursive imports; to our knowledge, no real language has these (perhaps for good reason)" (§2.4). Rouvoet 2020 §6 found that Rust implements imports that affect their own resolution, judged a Rust example ambiguous, and showed the sound scheduler gets stuck on such programs even when a stable model exists. That supports `defs.source.ud`'s judgment that self-influencing mediation is live in this domain, not exotic.

**Composition of cardinalities along a path.** `type-algebra.md` R4 observes that one {n,m} axis appears at four sites in udon — the suffix sugar, the paths arity bounds, schema cardinality, and the type exponents — and records the paths section's conjecture that bounds compose through nesting, with the weaker bound winning. When ADDRESS and DIRECTIONS are defined, intended cardinality composed step by step along a path is the place to test that.

**udon's own references are composite.** The primer §4.6: `@licence[mit].realized` recognizes as the selector (name?, key?, traits), and "Traits on a reference are *selection criteria*". The key part reaches through a policed binding; the traits part is a match. `def-reference` leaves "the internal composite structure of references" open; udon's selector is the first specimen to hand.

**From the demand map** (`addressing-exploration.md`): D9 (`at` = exactly one or error, `all` = explicitly plural) is intended cardinality declared at the API — direct demand evidence for putting it on the reference. Trap 3, "Confusing match multiplicity with value multiplicity — stacked `:fed-by` is not the same bug as two `|user[alice]`", is a distinction the theory does not yet make: plurality from several bindings reached vs plurality from one binding whose value is a stack. And the observation that agents mostly address relationally (`||type[key]` at any depth) is, in this theory's terms, a match over the document's population that behaves like a designator wherever (type, key) uniqueness is policed — "Kind follows maintenance", in the wild.
